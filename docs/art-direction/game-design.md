# Game design and technical handoff

Status: user-selected direct Trickster Online fan remake; PC combat vertical slice first, online world later. Bunny is the first hero candidate, with Coral Beach as the field reference and Megalopolis as the later town reference. This document specifies visual requirements, not an implementation or a claim that combat is balanced.

## Identity and modernization

The emotional target is **sunlit storybook adventure with a dangerous heartbeat**. Preserve cheerful adventurer identity, animal-associated class expression, whimsical environments, discovery through drilling, companion affection, collection and equipment growth, welcoming social places, and distinct combat roles. These are a memory-based interpretation of Trickster Online and require player/source verification; source availability is documented in the main research ledger. The user has chosen to retain recognizable Trickster identities and names. Generate fresh interpretations; do not extract or repurpose original game sprites, textures, maps, audio or logos.

Borrow Hades' principles of immediate response, strong posing, distinct attack phases, legible threat, and meaningful movement. Develop a coherent new rendering language, palette treatment and effects grammar while preserving recognizable Trickster places and adventurers. A gothic Greek overlay would lose the desired emotional identity. Combat intensity must alternate with calm exploration and discovery. Drill interaction is a central identity test, not decorative scenery.

Modern controls should support movement and aim as separate intentions, contextual interaction, readable cooldowns, and a small immediately accessible skill set. Mouse/keyboard and controller targeting need equivalent clarity. Input buffering, aim assistance, cancellation, dash invulnerability and lock-on are hypotheses to prototype, not established requirements copied from either reference. Replacing all positioning and commitment with repeated dashes could undermine class identity and pacing.

## Four visual class families

The table uses Trickster's Power/Magic/Sense/Charm families, remembered character associations, and new visual treatments. Historical character details remain pending source verification. Produce all four families as a comparative concept sheet; finish Bunny as the first playable candidate. Do not multiply a finished animation library across all four before the pipeline pilot passes.

| Family / working identity | Silhouette and material | Movement and action language | Signature art requirements | Risk to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Power / Bunny and Buffalo | Compact forward-weighted silhouette; prominent gauntlet or short broad weapon; warm cloth, matte metal, one bold animal-linked accessory | Planted compression, decisive arc, clear recoil; weight comes from pose and timing, not delayed input | Neutral, travel, ready, strike anticipation/contact/recovery, second strike, evade, hurt, defeat, drilling, discovery | Giant effects hiding the target or a huge weapon implying a matching collision area |
| Magic / Sheep and Dragon | Open triangular robe silhouette, lifted sleeves, identifiable focus; cool fabric with warm face and restrained luminous trim | Small directional steps, deliberate focus gesture, expansive cast followed by compact recovery | Focus and release poses, directional projectile origin, ground-target cue, persistent zone boundary, casting interruption | Glow becoming a constant visual fog; cloth replacing readable foot placement |
| Sense / Fox and Lion | Lean asymmetrical travel gear, satchel, lens and compact ranged implement; leather, ceramic and glass | Precise pointing, quick reposition, tool handling; distinct aim and movement directions | Aim, shot and recoil, optional reload concept, placed gadget, discovery inspection, drilling expertise expression | Blending with Power at thumbnail size; equipment changing sides between views |
| Charm / Cat and Raccoon | Broad rounded upper shape, protective mantle or shield, soft textiles against one strong hard surface | Open welcoming idle, grounded guarding, confident shove and rally gesture | Guard entry/hold/release, protective arc or zone, impact absorption, rally, companion interaction | Treating Charm solely as a cute appearance or a passive tank with no expressive agency |

Each family needs an uncolored silhouette, neutral material pass, eight-direction reference, one calm social pose, one action key pose, drilling pose, and comparison at gameplay size. Animal association should read through a coherent accessory/anatomy policy chosen by the art director; avoid an arbitrary mix of cosmetic ears and full animal anatomy. Retain recognizable Bunny/Buffalo, Sheep/Dragon, Fox/Lion and Cat/Raccoon associations for this direct remake; confirm exact original designs and class/progression details with trustworthy references before locking model sheets. The first pass uses Bunny's recognizable ears, athletic combat identity and glove-led pose language, without copying an existing sprite.

## First playable art slice

Bunny as one finished hero, one small Coral Beach-inspired exploration/combat field with a calm discovery edge, two enemy roles, one elite variation, a drilling site, one companion, and a concise skill UI. A concept presentation can show the four families and additional biomes without promising production-ready assets for them.

Enemy A is a close-range pursuer: low compact silhouette, directional lean before a committed strike, narrow ground wedge, visible recovery. Enemy B is a ranged controller: tall/open silhouette, visible charging appendage, a ground target marker or directional lane, projectile with a readable head and short tail. The elite must change silhouette and attack grammar as well as scale or color. Avoid authoring three entirely independent skeleton families before the animation feasibility test.

Future AI can combine perception, pursuit, retreat, windup, attack, recovery, interruption and return-to-home states. Every important state requires a visible cue. Enemies should have attack opportunities and coordination rules so the scene does not become overlapping telegraph noise. The renderer must never be responsible for deciding a hit; gameplay emits semantic events and visuals respond.

## Animation and combat contract

Use action phases `anticipation`, `contact`, `active`, `recovery`, `exit` as applicable. Author pose keys and metadata that identify these phases. Gameplay owns hit detection, damage windows, invulnerability and cancellation; frame numbers alone must not define simulation rules. A later timing change should not require repainting every frame. Frame count and playback rate are independent.

Prototype-only timing hypotheses: a light melee action may take roughly 0.30–0.50 seconds overall; an ordinary enemy's intentional warning may take about 0.45–0.80 seconds; a hero evade may last about 0.18–0.30 seconds. These are test ranges, not Hades measurements, balance specifications, required invulnerability windows or instructions to bake durations into art. Readability testing and the input prototype determine actual values. Use clear anticipation/contact/recovery even if animation playback is stylized and lower-rate than simulation.

Required hero states: idle, locomotion, ready/aim, primary attack, one skill, evade, hurt, defeat, interact, drilling and discovery. Add a second attack only if needed to prove transitions. Dedicated weapon trails, dust, hits, ground shadows and UI cues are separate layers. Avoid one giant image that bakes together character, shadow and effect.

Eight directional reference views are the target; the feasibility gate decides whether the production technique can consistently deliver them. Movement can use continuous vectors while visual facing uses eight directions. Aim facing may differ from travel facing. Test strafe/backpedal animation or an explicit turn/slide policy; direction references alone do not solve this motion. Normalize movement speed in the chosen simulation/world metric rather than assuming equal screen-vector magnitudes correspond to equal travel under the dimetric projection. Do not silently mirror asymmetric weapons or costumes in final assets. A four-direction reduced pilot is a conscious fallback requiring a visual comparison, not an invisible downgrade.

All actor frames share a fixed foot origin, scale, view projection, landmark proportions and light direction. Visual bounds, ground footprint and collision/hurtbox are separate concepts. Dynamic trims require per-frame offsets. A sword tip outside the ground footprint does not enlarge an actor's navigation collision. Tall foliage or roofs need separate occluding elements or a fade policy; ground and actor layers must allow feet-based sorting.

## Drilling and companion requirements

Drilling sequence: approach and orient → plant tool → steady drill loop → resistance/success feedback → reveal → retrieve/celebrate → return to neutral. Keep hands, shaft and ground contact coherent throughout. Provide a ground disturbance, small debris, an optional discovery sparkle, reward silhouette and concise progress feedback separately. Avoid flashy repeated reward bursts that drown out ordinary finds. Prototype whether combat can interrupt drilling and author a clean abort pose; do not assume every digging event is an invulnerable cutscene. Optional higher-value finds should use a stronger reveal grammar rather than a different camera projection.

Companion states: idle, follow, arrive, look-at/curiosity, affection/emote, discovery assist, and safe offscreen recovery behavior. Movement must not cover the hero's feet or hostile warnings. Companion effects use lower visual priority than enemy tells. Combat functionality, ownership, growth and multiplayer persistence remain later design choices. The art slice needs personality and readable companionship before a full pet economy.

## Readability review

Review both calm discovery and worst-case combat at actual display scale. Temporarily remove color: role, hero and hostile windup should remain distinguishable. Test color-vision simulations; threat uses shape, motion and placement as well as color. Enemy tells are most important immediately before contact; allied effects, loot, pet sparkles and ornamental particles must yield visual priority. Test edge-of-screen attacks and action behind tall props. Include an input/animation capture with foot-origin overlays and speed reference to catch sliding.

Reviewers should answer: Where am I? What is about to hit? Where is safe? What did my action hit? Which object can I drill or interact with? Does the calm scene still feel inviting? A beautiful still that cannot answer these in motion fails the production gate.

## Godot practical direction

Verified against the parent's cached Godot 4.6 documentation: AnimatedSprite2D accepts individual images or sprite sheets; AnimationPlayer can coordinate additional properties and can operate with AnimatedSprite2D. TileMapLayer supports layered maps, Y-sort origin, collision and navigation settings. 2D lighting supports normal/specular maps and occluders. Documentation snapshots are included in `research/sources/2d_sprite_animation.rst`, `using_tilemaps.rst`, and `2d_lights_and_shadows.rst`.

Design recommendations, not documentation guarantees: use native 2D world coordinates with an isometric-looking ground projection, feet-based actor sorting, separately authored collision/navigation data, and renderer-independent actor state. Decide projection, reference actor height, tile proportions, light direction and screen-space scale during the pilot. A 2:1 diamond convention is an available production choice, not a universal requirement or proof that a camera is mathematically isometric. Avoid full normal-map production until a sample proves it improves painted forms rather than flattening or doubling baked lighting. Runtime target, texture memory and shader use require measurement before asset multiplication.
